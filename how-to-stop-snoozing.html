How to stop snoozing your alarm (for good)

Snoozing feels like extra rest, but those nine-minute chunks are broken, light sleep. The fix isn't more willpower at 5:30 AM. It's setting things up so snoozing isn't possible.

Swipe the story →

Why snoozing makes mornings harder

Each time you snooze, you drift back into light sleep and get pulled out again a few minutes later. You restart waking up several times instead of once, which is why a snoozed morning often feels worse than getting up at the first alarm. It also teaches your brain that the first alarm doesn't count.

6 steps to stop snoozing

1. Remove the snooze button

Use an alarm with no snooze at all. If the button exists, a sleepy brain will find it.

2. Move the off switch away from the bed

Make the alarm stop only when you're up and walking, for example with tags around your home that you have to reach. See no-snooze alarm apps.

3. Make the first steps tiny

"Get up" is a big decision. "Walk to the door" is a small one. Break the first five minutes into small wins:

  1. Bedroom door: you're out of bed. That's the hardest win, done.
  2. Fish tank: a small job (feed them) that needs your eyes open.
  3. Stair corner: your body is moving now.
  4. Mushroom painting: lights on, eyes properly open.
  5. Sink: brushing. By now you're awake, and the alarm stops.

4. Same wake time every day

Your body clock settles into a rhythm, and the alarm starts to feel less like a shock.

5. Give the morning a reason

Put your last stop on something you want: filter coffee, your running shoes, ten quiet minutes. Getting up is easier when there's something waiting.

6. Never miss twice

You'll have a bad morning. The rule is to make sure the next one counts. A streak calendar helps you see it.

Questions

Is snoozing bad for you?

Snoozing gives you fragmented, light sleep rather than real rest, and many people feel groggier after it. Getting up at the first alarm is usually better.

How long does it take to stop snoozing?

Many people notice a difference within a week or two of a fixed wake time and no snooze option. A 21-day stretch is a good target.
