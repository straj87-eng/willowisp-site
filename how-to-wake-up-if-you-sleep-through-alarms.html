How to wake up if you sleep through alarms

For years I switched off my alarm in my sleep and had no memory of doing it. Here's why that happens, and the fixes that finally worked, starting with the one that matters most: move the off switch out of reach.

Why you sleep through alarms (or switch them off)

When an alarm wakes you, part of your brain is still asleep for a while. This groggy state is called sleep inertia. Your hand can find the phone and swipe, but the part of you that makes plans and remembers things isn't fully online yet. So you switch it off and forget.

That's why "just try harder" doesn't work. The decision is made by a brain that isn't fully awake.

7 fixes that work

1. Put the off switch outside the bedroom

If you can switch the alarm off from bed, you will. Make the only off switch something you reach on your feet: a tag on the door, in the bathroom or in the kitchen. This is the single biggest change.

2. Keep moving for a few minutes

One trip across the room isn't enough; it's easy to walk back to bed. A short route of steps keeps you moving until sleep inertia fades. That's why I use five tags, not one:

  1. Bedroom door: you're out of bed. That's the hardest win, done.
  2. Fish tank: a small job (feed them) that needs your eyes open.
  3. Stair corner: your body is moving now.
  4. Mushroom painting: lights on, eyes properly open.
  5. Sink: brushing. By now you're awake, and the alarm stops.

3. Wake at the same time every day

A fixed wake time, weekends included, helps your body clock expect the morning. Sleeping in on Saturday makes Monday feel like jet lag.

4. Get light in the first ten minutes

Open the curtains or switch the lights on straight away. Light is one of the strongest "it's morning" signals for your body clock.

5. Use one alarm, not six

Many alarms teach you that the first one doesn't count. One alarm you can't dismiss from bed beats six you can.

6. Fix the night before

Phone charging away from the bed, bedtime 15–20 minutes earlier if you're short on sleep. The morning is often lost the night before.

7. Don't negotiate in bed

Swipe, stand, walk. Decide whether you "really need" to get up once you're at the sink, not under the blanket.

When to see a doctor

If you regularly sleep through very loud alarms, or feel exhausted after a full night, talk to a doctor. Conditions like sleep apnoea or insomnia can cause this, and no alarm can fix them.

Questions

Why do I turn off my alarm without remembering?

Sleep inertia: right after waking, parts of your brain responsible for memory and decisions are still waking up, so you can act without remembering it.

Is putting the phone across the room enough?

It helps for a few steps, but many people walk back to bed. A route of several stops, ideally outside the bedroom, works better.
